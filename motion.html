LESSON 01

Motion Don't Memorize It. Understand It.

Motion is not just about objects moving. It is about describing exactly where an object is, how its position changes, and how that change happens with time.

👁️ SEE IT 🧠 UNDERSTAND IT 📐 DERIVE IT ✍️ APPLY IT

START HERE

What exactly is motion?

Imagine you are standing beside a road. A car is parked beside you.

One minute later, the car is 100 metres away from you.

Something has changed: the position of the car.

💡
Core idea

An object is in motion when its position changes with time relative to a reference point.

🚗

Same car. Different position. Time has passed.

Think like a physicist: Instead of simply saying “the car is moving,” ask: “How has its position changed with time?”
02

HOW MUCH GROUND?

Distance

Distance tells us the total length of the path travelled by an object.

📏

Distance is a scalar quantity.

A scalar quantity has magnitude only. Distance tells us how much ground was covered, but it does not tell us which direction the object travelled.

A
50 m →
B
30 m →
C

Total distance:

50 m + 30 m = 80 m
Remember: Distance cares about the whole journey.
03

DIRECTION MATTERS

Displacement

Displacement is the change in position of an object in a specified direction.

🧭

Displacement is a vector quantity.

A vector has both magnitude and direction. Displacement therefore describes where an object ended up compared with where it started.

START
→
100 m EAST
←
100 m WEST
●

The person travelled 200 m in total, but finished where they started.

DISTANCE 200 m
DISPLACEMENT 0 m
⚠️
Exam trap

Distance and displacement are not always the same. A round trip can have a large distance but zero displacement.

04

HOW FAST?

Speed

Speed tells us how quickly distance is being covered.

SPEED = DISTANCE ÷ TIME
Distance How much ground?
Time How long?
Speed How quickly?

WORKED EXAMPLE

A cyclist travels 120 m in 10 seconds. Find the speed.

Step 1 — Write the relationship

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Step 2 — Substitute the values

Speed = 120 ÷ 10

Step 3 — Calculate

Speed = 12 m/s
ANSWER: 12 m/s
05

SPEED + DIRECTION

Velocity

Velocity tells us the rate at which displacement changes with time.

➡️

Velocity is a vector quantity.

Saying that a car is travelling at 20 m/s tells us how fast it is moving. Saying it is travelling at 20 m/s east tells us both its speed and direction.

SPEED 20 m/s No direction
VELOCITY 20 m/s east Magnitude + direction
06

CHANGE IN VELOCITY

Acceleration

Acceleration tells us how quickly velocity changes with time.

🚀

Acceleration does not simply mean “going faster.”

An object accelerates whenever its velocity changes. That can mean:

  • speeding up
  • slowing down
  • changing direction

🚗 faster
→
🚗 fast
🧠
Key idea

Acceleration measures the change in velocity, not simply the velocity itself.

07

DON'T MEMORIZE — DERIVE

Where Do the Equations of Motion Come From?

The equations of motion are not random formulas invented for students to memorize. They come directly from our definitions of acceleration, velocity and displacement.

DERIVATION 01

Deriving v = u + at

Start with the definition of acceleration:

a = (v − u) / t

Multiply both sides by t:

at = v − u

Add u to both sides:

v = u + at
What did we just prove?

The final velocity equals the initial velocity plus the change in velocity produced by acceleration over time.

THE GRAPH TELLS THE STORY

Why does the velocity-time graph matter?

For constant acceleration, velocity increases uniformly with time.

Velocity
Time
u v

The area underneath a velocity-time graph represents displacement.

DERIVATION 02

Deriving s = ut + ½at²

Look at the velocity-time graph. The area under the graph is the displacement.

The area can be separated into:

Rectangle ut
Triangle ½(v − u)t

Therefore:

s = ut + ½(v − u)t

From the first equation:

v − u = at

Substitute:

s = ut + ½(at)t
s = ut + ½at²
The important idea:

We did not memorize this equation. We obtained it from the meaning of displacement and acceleration.

DERIVATION 03

Deriving v² = u² + 2as

Start with:

v = u + at

From the displacement equation:

s = ½(u + v)t

From the first equation:

t = (v − u) / a

Substitute this into the displacement equation:

s = ½(u + v)(v − u) / a

Multiply both sides by 2a:

2as = (u + v)(v − u)

Using the difference of two squares:

2as = v² − u²

Rearranging:

v² = u² + 2as

KNOW YOUR SYMBOLS

What does each symbol mean?

v Final velocity
a Acceleration
t Time
s Displacement
08

SEE MOTION

Motion Through Graphs

Graphs turn motion into a picture. Instead of only reading numbers, we can look at the graph and immediately see how an object behaves.

Distance-Time Graph

The slope of a distance-time graph tells us the speed.

Steeper line → greater speed

Velocity-Time Graph

The slope tells us acceleration, while the area under the graph gives displacement.

Slope → acceleration

WASSCE-STYLE THINKING

Let's put the ideas together.

Question

A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 4 m/s² for 5 seconds. Find its final velocity.

1 Identify the information.
2 Choose the appropriate equation.
3 Substitute carefully.
4 Give the correct unit.

Given:

u = 0 m/s a = 4 m/s² t = 5 s

Use:

v = u + at

Therefore:

v = 0 + (4 × 5)
v = 20 m/s
